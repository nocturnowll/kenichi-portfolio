## The Problem

Confiction Labs was launching RIFTSTORM — a consumer gaming product with no established brand and a global audience to reach from day one. In gaming, a quiet launch is a failed launch.

## What I Did

I owned two of the most critical acquisition and retention levers: **community** and **creator partnerships**.

On community: I built the Discord from near-zero to 15,000+ members. The engagement architecture — tournaments, AMAs, giveaways, creator-led content, regional events — kept members active between launches and turned them into the launch's loudest advocates.

On partnerships: I identified and directed 7+ global creator and influencer partnerships across US, EU, and SEA. These drove discovery in markets where we had no existing presence.

I also represented the brand at major gaming conferences — PAX East, PAX West, Gamescom, Token 2049 — handling live demos, press interviews, and partner acquisition on the floor.

When the fundraise came around, I contributed to the deck alongside the founders and C-suite. The community metrics and retention data I'd built were part of what investors saw.

## The Outcome

- **110,590 downloads** across 130 countries within 72 hours of launch
- **#1 in category** on launch day
- **67.16% D1 / 33.20% D7 retention** — strong for the category
- **1,270 peak concurrent users**
- **15,000+ community members** sustained post-launch
- **$6.5M funding round** closed — community traction was part of the story

## What I'd Do Differently With Today's Tools

The community management was largely manual. With the systems I build now, I'd automate the listening layer — sentiment tracking, FAQ routing, power-user identification — and focus human attention on the conversations that actually move the needle.
